Before booking an appointment, patients search. They look up the doctor's name, check the address and want to know whether their health plan is accepted. Often they find only an old social media profile, or nothing at all.

A practice website looks like an ordinary landing page, but it isn't. In Brazil, doctors and dentists follow their own advertising rules, and the website handles health data. To show how to bring all of this together on a page that is both beautiful and compliant, we built four templates with fictional professionals (the templates are in Portuguese):

- [Dermatology](https://modelos.attodev.com.br/)
- [Cardiology](https://modelos.attodev.com.br/cardiologia)
- [Pediatrics](https://modelos.attodev.com.br/pediatria)
- [Dentistry](https://modelos.attodev.com.br/odontologia)

This article explains the rules behind each decision.

## Who provides care, with no fine print

For doctors, Federal Council of Medicine (CFM) Resolution No. 2,336/2023 requires the ad to include the name, the word <span lang="pt-BR">MÉDICO</span> or <span lang="pt-BR">MÉDICA</span>, the CRM registration number with the state and, for each specialty advertised, the RQE — the specialist qualification registration. There can be at most two specialties, and this information appears in the same font, size and color.

For dentists, the Dental Code of Ethics requires the name, the council registration number and the full name of the profession: <span lang="pt-BR">cirurgião-dentista</span> (dental surgeon). A specialty can only be advertised if it is registered with the regional dental council (CRO). Dentists who are not specialists can present themselves as general practitioners.

In the templates, this identification block appears right at the top of the page, next to the contact buttons, and is repeated at the end, in a stamp, and in the footer.

## Prices: allowed for doctors, not for dentists

The most surprising difference is about prices. Under CFM Resolution No. 2,336/2023, in force since March 2024, doctors can publish the consultation fee and payment methods. The price of procedures stays out of the ad and is agreed with the patient.

In dentistry, the rule is the opposite. The Dental Code of Ethics and Law No. 5,081/1966 prohibit advertising prices, payment terms and free services. No assessment fee, no installments, no "free assessment". The address, phone number, opening hours and accepted health plans are still allowed.

That's why, in the dentistry template, the pricing block became <span lang="pt-BR">"Convênios e orçamento"</span> (health plans and estimate): the estimate is delivered in writing after the assessment, before any treatment.

## What stays off the website

- **Promises and superlatives.** "The best", "the only", "guaranteed results" and "free" stay out in both professions, as does sensationalism.
- **Recognizable patients.** The Code of Medical Ethics forbids showing a patient, or an image that makes them recognizable, in professional advertising, even with authorization. In the template photos, patients appear from behind, and a child's face never appears.
- **Before and after.** The CFM only allows it for educational purposes and under strict rules. The CFO accepts only the dentist's own cases, with a consent form and the name and registration number on the image. The templates don't have this section.
- **Procedures in progress.** In dentistry, photos of treatment in progress cannot be published. The template photos show the dentist explaining with a dental arch model, reading an X-ray and the empty office.
- **Testimonials.** Instead, the templates list concrete commitments (appointments on time, written instructions, privacy) and a link to the Google reviews.

So as not to rely on the writer's memory, the templates have an automated test. If a text uses a banned term, advertises a price on a dentist's site or puts a symptom in the WhatsApp message, the build fails and the site isn't published.

## Educational content, with sources

The section that helps patients the most is also the one that demands the most care. Each template has one, sober and with its source cited:

- **Dermatology:** the ABCDE rule for checking moles at home, from the Brazilian Society of Dermatology.
- **Cardiology:** the blood pressure ranges from the 2025 Brazilian Guideline on Arterial Hypertension, on a gauge that follows the scroll.
- **Pediatrics:** the routine checkup schedule from the Ministry of Health's Child Health Booklet, with a growth ruler that rises to the WHO median height at each stage.
- **Dentistry:** how a cavity advances from the enamel to the pulp, based on the Ministry of Health's oral health material.

The tone avoids fear. The section explains what to watch for and when to seek care, without promising results.

## The LGPD starts at the WhatsApp button

The booking button opens WhatsApp with a ready-made message. Since that message travels in the link's address, it carries no symptom or health data — only the booking request. In the pediatrics template, it doesn't carry the child's name or age either.

The site has no form, no ad pixel and no advertising cookies. The privacy policy says who controls the data, what it is used for, who it is shared with and how long it is kept. In pediatrics, it explains that children's data depends on the consent of a parent or legal guardian, as required by article 14 of the LGPD, Brazil's data protection law.

The top of each template has four styles to choose from. The default is a lens that follows the cursor and shows what the specialist sees: the skin under the dermatoscope, the heart under the stethoscope, the growth curve on the ruler, the X-ray behind the smile. On mobile, it moves around on its own and goes wherever you tap. The other three are the practice's letterhead, with the council stamp; a typographic street-poster style; and a schedule that shows, in Brasília time, whether the practice is open now and when it opens next. You can switch between them in <span lang="pt-BR">"Modelos e estilos"</span> (templates and styles), in the bar at the top of each template.

Names, registrations and photos are fictional. The images were generated with AI for the demo; on a client's site, real photos of the professional and the practice go in.

Under the hood, each template is a static site, fast on mobile, with animations that respect people who prefer less motion and accessibility checked page by page.
